3D models nobody can take down.

Swamp is a peer-to-peer library of printable models. No company in the middle, no accounts, no takedown button, no algorithm deciding what you see. Just people, files and a lot of mud.

Publish a model and it sticks. Search everything without anyone watching what you search for. Find out who really made the thing you're about to print.

Prototype. It works on our machines and on the Logos test network. It has not met the real world, it has no audit, and the test network resets. Every file is hash-checked, so it shouldn't eat your STLs, but we wouldn't bet the farm. Or the pond.

Why a swamp?

Model sites live in towers: one company, one database, one moderation team, one bad quarter away from a paywall. Swamps don't have towers. They're messy, they're everywhere at once, and things that live there are very hard to get rid of.

StickyPublished versions are signed, content-addressed and mirrored by anyone who wants to. You can't edit history and nobody can quietly delete it. Retract your own model and it hides, but it doesn't vanish from the people who have it.
MurkyBrowse the categories you follow from your own copy: offline, and nobody learns what you look at. Search everything else by fetching only the index piece your query needs (privately over a mixnet, once the network's mixnet works).
No landlordNo accounts. Your identity is a key on your device. Anyone can run a hub, and no hub is in charge.
TeemingRemixes link to what they remix. Makes come with photos. Comments, likes, versions. Everything Printables has, minus the company.

How it works

A desktop app for Logos Basecamp. Under the hood: signed event logs over Logos Messaging, files in Logos Storage, and soon priority proofs anchored on LEZ, the Logos execution zone.

Publish

Drop in your STL and photos. Swamp hashes every file, renders a thumbnail and takes a shape fingerprint. The listing goes out at once, and files upload in the background. Versions are immutable: improve it, publish v2.

Follow categories

Your node keeps a full copy of the categories you follow, like Tools, Toys and Gridfinity, and nothing else. Fast, offline and private, and it doesn't need a data centre.

Search everywhere

Indexing hubs publish signed, deterministic index snapshots. You fetch only the piece your query needs and rank results yourself. Two honest indexers produce identical bytes, so leaving something out shows.

Hubs, not landlords

Anyone can run a hub to keep files alive and answer searches. A hub may refuse content, but it must say what it drops and why. Declared exclusion is a policy, silent omission is provable cheating, and you pick your hubs.

Stolen models

Every version carries a shape fingerprint from day one. Next up: "possible copy of X by Y, published earlier", with evidence your client re-checks itself. Labels inform. They never delete anything.

Remix, make, repeat

Remixes credit and link the original (and warn you when its licence says no derivatives). Post your make with a photo. Download is verified byte for byte, then it goes into your slicer.

"Unregulated", honestly

There is no central moderator, and there never will be. That doesn't mean anything goes. It means nobody can decide for everyone.

Who decides what I see?

You do. Your client verifies every signature and ranks everything locally. Hubs choose what they carry and must publish their exclusion lists. Labelers, coming later, are things you subscribe to, not authorities.

Can a hub censor a model?

It can refuse to carry it, and it has to say so. It can't forge entries, can't edit them and can't rank them for you. If it signs an index that silently drops a model published before that index, the hub has signed the proof of its own cheating, and clients stop trusting it. If every hub colludes, someone starts a new hub: it's one command.

What about stolen designs?

That's the problem we care most about. Exact copies are detected 95-100% of the time in our benchmark; edited copies are still hard and we say so. The plan is evidence-carrying labels plus "who published first" proofs anchored on chain. We can't stop theft, but we can make sure people can find the original.

Is it legal?

Swamp is a tool. What you publish, carry or print is your business, under your local laws.

Phones?

Later. 3D printing happens at a desk; the desktop app comes first and has to be good.

Get into the swamp

Swamp runs inside Logos Basecamp 0.3, on Linux (x86-64).

  1. Install Basecamp 0.3.1. A separate profile keeps your other apps out of the experiment: HOME=$HOME/swamp-profile ./Basecamp-0.3.1.AppImage
  2. In Basecamp, open the Package Manager, add this repository and install Swamp. Its dependencies come along.
    https://apps.vpavlin.xyz/basecamp/logos-repo.json
  3. Open Swamp, set a name under Me, pick the categories you care about and wade in.
  4. To print, install OrcaSlicer 2.4+ or Bambu Studio. "Open in slicer" works with any printer. A Bambu Lab A1 / A1 mini in LAN-only + Developer Mode can print straight from Swamp, over your own network.
  5. Got a swamp://model/... link from someone? Paste it into the search box.

Where the swamp is at

  1. Shape fingerprints and a benchmark. Whole-object copies detected 95-100% at 0.1% false positives.
  2. Publish, browse, versions, remixes, makes, comments, likes, verified downloads. Tested headless and in Basecamp 0.3.1 on the Logos test network.
  3. Follow categories instead of mirroring everything. Lazy pictures.
  4. Global search over signed index snapshots. Proven on the Logos test network: found, opened and downloaded a model from a category the node doesn't follow. Private (mixnet) fetching is built, but switched off until the network's mixnet works.
  5. Creators audit the indexers. A hub that silently leaves your model out gets caught with its own signature; a declared exclusion is shown as policy.
  6. Open in your slicer, or print straight to a Bambu Lab A1 over your LAN. Sliced on your machine with the printer's own profile; geometry only, so nobody else's G-code reaches your printer. No cloud, no account.
  7. Share links: send someone a model, they paste it into Swamp.
  8. An always-on hub, so two people behind home routers can share files.
  9. Copy labels for exact and whole-object copies.
  10. "Who published first" anchored on LEZ.
  11. Live anonymous query hubs, and sharing evidence between nodes.
  12. Edited-copy detection, labelers and disputes. Tips.
