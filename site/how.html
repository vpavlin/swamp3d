How Swamp works

A 3D-model library with no company in the middle, on the Logos stack. Diagrams for explaining it.

1 · The big picture built

Two Logos services do the work. Messaging carries small, signed events: "this model exists", "here's version 2", a like, a comment. Storage carries the files, addressed by their content. Your device keeps its own copy of the catalogue for the categories you follow. Hubs are just Swamp running on an always-on server: they keep copies so the network works when your laptop sleeps.

2 · What happens when you publish built

Your app does the heavy lifting locally: it hashes every file, renders a thumbnail and takes a shape fingerprint (for spotting copies later). The listing goes out over Messaging at once; the files upload to Storage in the background. A hub sees the new model, pulls a copy while your computer is online, and from then on serves it to everyone.

3 · How we use Logos Messaging built

The catalogue is an append-only log of signed events, not a database. Each category is its own topic, so your node only follows what you care about and keeps a full copy of it: browsing is local, works offline, and nobody sees what you look at. When you come back online, your node compares what it has with its peers (a set-reconciliation catch-up) and fetches only the missing events. Every node checks every signature, and only a model's creator can add versions to it.

4 · How we use Logos Storage, and why there are hubs built

Storage finds files by asking a DHT "who holds this CID?" and then connects to a holder directly. Most of us sit behind home routers (NAT): nobody can connect to us. Two home computers can only reach each other through a relay, and relays are capped (about 128 KB and 2 minutes per connection), so big files between two homes stall. A hub is reachable, so the publisher's computer connects out to it, the hub pulls the file, and everyone else downloads from the hub.

5 · Search: the index built

You can't search what you don't follow without asking somebody. Swamp's answer: indexers (hubs, or anyone with the full catalogue) publish a signed, deterministic index, split into small shards by term prefix. To search for "vase", you fetch only the shard for "vas…" and rank the results yourself. Because the index is deterministic, two honest indexers produce identical bytes, and if one quietly leaves a model out, its creator notices and holds a signed proof.

6 · Privacy: what's private and what isn't partly

Swamp is a public library: everything you publish is signed and permanent. The privacy story is about what you look at and download. Browsing your own categories is fully private. Searching and downloading still leak something today, mostly because the network's mixnet isn't working yet.

7 · What the blockchain is for (LEZ) planned

Swamp works without a chain today. We'd add LEZ, the Logos execution zone, for two things a log of signatures can't do alone. Time: proving when something existed, so "who published first" holds up when someone copies a model. And money: tips first, paid models later. Only small roots and payments go on chain; models, files and the index stay off it.
